The differentiator — six creative disciplines

Sound
Sound artist / composer
The group builds a piece of music together — no instruments played before, no score required. A composer leads from raw material: voice, rhythm, found objects. The session produces a recording the team takes home.
Develops: listening without hierarchy · non-verbal signal
Move
Choreographer / movement director
A choreographer works with the group on spatial composition and physical ensemble decision-making. No dance experience required. The session uses movement to surface dynamics that language obscures.
Develops: spatial awareness · trust · ensemble response
Sing
Choral director — Georgian polyphony
Georgian polyphonic chant is a UNESCO intangible heritage form — three independent voices that must coexist without a conductor. A choral director leads the group through learning and performing a piece.
Develops: holding your part · vocal presence · group coherence
Build
Ceramicist — FORGE workshop
A session in RUNO's in-house fabrication workshop. The group works with clay or structural materials to produce a physical object. The result is real — it can be fired, finished, and sent to the office.
Develops: physical problem-solving · tolerance for process
See
Filmmaker / visual artist
A filmmaker leads the group through a location-based visual exercise in Tbilisi — Old Town, Mtatsminda, or the industrial districts. The session is about framing, not photography: what you notice, what you cut.
Develops: observation · framing · attention to detail
Write
Writer / editor
A structured writing session followed by group reading. No literary ambition required — the prompts are specific, the time is short. What emerges is usually the most surprising session of the four days.
Develops: precision of expression · willingness to be specific

The output

Not a certificate.
Something they
actually made.

Every discipline produces a physical or tangible result. The sessions are not workshops in the standard sense — they are short residencies, and residencies have outputs.

The reason this matters: a team that made something together has a different reference point from a team that attended something together. The memory is attached to effort, not to passive experience.

A recording
From the Sound session — a piece of music the group composed and performed together, mixed and sent to the team after the programme.
A ceramic object
From the Build session — a physical piece made in FORGE. Can be fired and glazed, then shipped to the office or distributed among team members.
A short film
From the See session — edited footage from the Tbilisi location exercise, with each participant contributing their own frame.
A text
From the Write session — a collection of pieces from the group, compiled and printed before the final dinner. A document that did not exist before they arrived.
