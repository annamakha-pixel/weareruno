*Last updated: 2026-04-11*

## Product Overview

**One-liner:**
A full-service 4-day corporate offsite in Georgia — logistics handled by RUNO, afternoons built around six creative or sport disciplines led by Tbilisi-based practitioners.

**What it does:**
RUNO produces corporate offsites in Tbilisi from brief to debrief: venue, accommodation, meals, transport, working session setup, and an afternoon programme of six discipline sessions (art or sport track, or mixed). The team flies in. Everything else is already in place. The format is designed to produce three specific outcomes: the team comes back rested, with a skill they didn't have before, and slightly different in a way that shows up in how they work.

**Product category:**
Corporate offsite / team experience / HR travel programme
(How buyers search: "corporate offsite Georgia", "team retreat abroad", "company offsite Europe", "HR team experience")

**Product type:**
B2B service, fully bespoke per group

**Business model:**
From $900 per person, all-inclusive. Minimum 30 people. Price varies by venue, group size, accommodation standard, and track configuration. International flights not included.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:**
Standard offsite formats (workshop + dinner + activity) produce no lasting effect. The team comes back, opens the shared folder once, and returns to exactly the same dynamic. HR leaders know this and are looking for what comes after — but the market hasn't given them a credible alternative in the same price range.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Barcelona/Lisbon/Amsterdam retreats: familiar, logistically competent, culturally empty. The place adds nothing.
- Luxury retreat providers (Swiss Alps, Tuscany): expensive, often over-designed, feel like a hotel package not a programme.
- Activity companies (cooking class, axe-throwing, escape room): a one-evening format, not a multi-day programme. Doesn't change the team dynamic.
- DIY with a local DMC: logistics stress falls back on the HR team. No programme design expertise.

**What it costs them:**
- Time: HR leads spend 40–80 hours coordinating a DIY offsite
- Money: $600–1,500/person for European alternatives, often without full programme design
- Opportunity: a poorly designed offsite actively damages morale ("we spent 4 days in a conference room in Marbella")

**Emotional tension:**
The HR leader carries personal reputational risk. If the team comes back unchanged, or worse — complaining — that's on them. They want to be the person who found something real. They are afraid of looking naive if they pitch something "artsy." They are afraid of looking uncreative if they book the same Barcelona package again.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Local Georgian DMCs and event agencies — know Tbilisi logistics, don't have a programme design capability or cultural production background. Can organise transfers and hotel. Cannot design a 4-day experience with practitioners.

**Secondary:** European boutique retreat providers (Portugal, Italy, Spain) — competent logistics, some programme design, but no Georgian cultural specificity. $1,200–2,000/person range. The "creative" element is usually a cooking class or wine tasting.

**Indirect:** Doing nothing / repeating last year's format — the most common "competitor." HR leaders default to familiar formats because the alternative is unclear. RUNO must make the alternative clear and de-risked.

**Indirect 2:** Internal coordination — HR + local hotel + external facilitator + activity company stitched together. Cheaper in theory, expensive in time and stress. No single point of accountability.

**Key differentiators:**
- FORGE: in-house fabrication workshop. The BUILD discipline is physically impossible to replicate without it.
- SING discipline: a group vocal session led by a choral director. Not a performance class — about listening, ensemble, and holding your part.
- Anna's background: 15 years in cultural production, Bolshoi, State of Qatar reception. This is not an events company that added a "creative programme."
- Full-service single point of contact: Yulia handles all production. The client makes one call.
- Location expertise: RUNO lives and works in Tbilisi. Knows venues not in any directory, practitioners who don't market themselves.

**How we do it differently:**
Start with the question "what should this team be able to do differently in six months?" and work backwards. Logistics follow from the answer, not the other way around.

**Why that's better:**
The outcome is designed in, not hoped for. The three specific outcomes (rest / skill / changed dynamic) are the brief — not a side effect.

**Why customers choose RUNO:**
No other provider in Tbilisi combines cultural production expertise + physical fabrication workshop + full operational logistics + international client experience (luxury, diplomatic, automotive).

## Switching Dynamics (JTBD Four Forces)

**Push** (what drives them away from current solution):
"We did Barcelona again and half the team was on their laptops by day two." Standard formats have lost credibility. The team has started calling offsites "mandatory fun." The HR leader is tired of defending a format they don't believe in.

**Pull** (what attracts them to RUNO):
A programme with a real structure and a named cultural producer behind it. Georgia as a destination that is genuinely different. The specificity of the outcomes — not "team bonding" but "a recording they made together, an object they built."

**Habit** (what keeps them with current approach):
"We know what we're getting." Familiar European city = predictable quality. The CEO knows what Barcelona is. Pitching Tbilisi requires explanation. Default = safe.

**Anxiety** (what worries them about switching):
- "What if the team hates it?"
- "What if the logistics go wrong in a country we don't know?"
- "What if the creative sessions are awkward and forced?"
- "How do I explain this to the CFO?"
